If you're watching every dollar, the official Quito airport shuttle bus is the budget champion. But "cheapest" and "best for your trip" aren't always the same thing. Here's the honest comparison.

## How the airport bus works

The official airport shuttle connects UIO with a couple of **designated stops** in the Quito area. The appeal is obvious — it's by far the cheapest way in. The catches:

- It drops you at a **fixed stop, not your hotel**, so you still need a taxi or rideshare for the final leg — with your luggage, after a long flight.
- It runs on a **schedule**, which may not line up with a late or delayed arrival.
- There's no help with bags and no one waiting for you.

It's a solid option for budget travelers arriving in daylight, traveling light, who don't mind a second connection at the end.

## How a private transfer compares

A private transfer is the opposite trade-off: more money, far less friction.

- **Door-to-door** to your exact hotel, no second leg.
- A **prepaid USD price** fixed before you fly — $50 to Wyndham Quito Airport, $75 to Cumbayá, $100 anywhere in Quito. See the [rates](/#rates).
- A vetted driver **waiting at arrivals** with your name, whenever you land, because we track your flight.

## Cost vs. everything else

The bus wins decisively on price. The transfer wins on **time, comfort, and certainty** — especially with luggage, kids, a night arrival, or a hotel that's awkward to reach from a bus stop.

## How to decide

- **Take the bus** if budget is the priority, it's daytime, you're traveling light, and your hotel is near a stop.
- **Take a private transfer** if you want to skip the connection entirely and arrive without stress — see [every option compared](/guide/getting-from-quito-airport-to-city), or how [families](/guide/quito-airport-transfer-for-families) handle the airport leg.

Both are valid. It comes down to whether you're optimizing for the lowest fare or the easiest arrival.
