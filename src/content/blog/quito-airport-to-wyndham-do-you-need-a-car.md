Wyndham Quito Airport is the hotel in Tababela next to Mariscal Sucre (UIO). Travelers book it for one reason: the flight is early, late, or both, and they do not want an hour on the highway before they sleep. The question that follows is whether you need a car at all.

You do not need a rental car for a night at this hotel. You do need a ride from the terminal to the door.

## How close it actually is

The prepaid transfer is typically **10–15 minutes** from arrivals to the hotel. That is the short product on this site. Cumbayá is 25–35 minutes. A Quito address is about 45–60 minutes. The comparison is in [how far Quito airport is](/guide/how-far-is-quito-airport-from-the-city).

"Next to the airport" is the right description and it is easy to over-read. Ten to fifteen minutes in a car is close beside a drive into the city. It is not a walk from the arrivals curb to a lobby with suitcases, children, or a laptop bag after a red-eye. We are not publishing a walking time or a distance on foot, because that is not how guests with luggage should plan the arrival. If a map app shows you a pedestrian route, treat it as a curiosity, not as the plan.

The $50 rate is only this hotel. A different Wyndham in Quito city — if you have booked one — is the **$100** anywhere-in-Quito rate. The names look similar on a confirmation email. The drives are not similar. Check the words "Quito Airport" and the Tababela address before you pay for the transfer.

## A rental car is the wrong tool for this hop

Renting a car at UIO makes sense for some longer Ecuador road trips. It does not make sense as the way to reach a hotel a few minutes from the terminal, sleep, and fly again. You would be handling a rental desk while tired, parking at the hotel, and returning the car before the outbound flight. The wider trade-off is in [renting a car versus a private transfer](/guide/renting-a-car-vs-private-transfer-quito).

For this stay, the vehicle you want is a car with a driver for the two short legs: inbound, and back to the terminal when you leave. Round trip is twice the one-way rate, so both directions are **$50 + $50** if you add the return when you book.

Some airport hotels run a shuttle of their own. We do not operate that shuttle, and we will not guess the hours, the fare, or whether it meets every flight. Ask Wyndham directly, on a current page or at the hotel, if a shuttle is part of your decision. Shuttle details change. If you want a named driver who is tracking your flight and waiting after midnight, book the transfer. The two can both exist; they are not the same service.

## When the $50 SUV is worth booking

The ride is short. The reasons to prepay it are the same reasons people prepay the longer city ride, compressed into a smaller distance.

- You are landing late. Many US flights reach UIO in the evening or after midnight. Standing outside to sort a car is the part of a 10-minute hop that takes the time. See [arriving late](/guide/arriving-quito-airport-late-at-night).
- You have bags, kids, or both. The driver meets you in the arrivals hall with your name and helps load the SUV. [Meet and greet](/guide/dont-arrive-to-an-empty-airport) is the whole ceremony. It is short, and it is the point.
- You do not want a rental, a rideshare pin, or a fare discussion for a hotel you can nearly see from the airport area.

An official taxi can also cover ten minutes, and sometimes for less money. That is a fair option if the rank is straightforward and you are comfortable with it. The prepaid SUV costs **$50** because the driver is licensed and airport-authorized, the price is already paid, and a delay does not restart the clock. We are not arguing the taxi is unsafe. We are describing a different product. The night version of that choice is [taxi or Uber from UIO after dark](/guide/taxi-or-uber-from-quito-airport-at-night).
