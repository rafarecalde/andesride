## The hall at a quarter to midnight

International arrivals go through immigration, then bags, then the exit. At 11:40pm the room is not the afternoon crush and it is not abandoned. You can still wait. A single delayed inbound ahead of you changes the line more than the printed schedule does. Passport out, form filled if the crew gave you one, liquids and laptop where you can reach them if anyone asks. None of that is complicated. Doing it while walking is how people lose the thread.

Checked bags: stay at the belt. The exit is where strangers offer rides. Your transfer, if you booked one, is a person with your name, past the doors — see [how meet and greet works](/guide/dont-arrive-to-an-empty-airport). If you are comparing that with a curb taxi, the plain version is [what a taxi from UIO actually involves](/guide/how-much-is-a-taxi-from-quito-airport).

## Thirty-five to forty-five minutes is the nighttime city drive

Tababela to Quito on Ruta Viva, once traffic has died down, is commonly about **35–45 minutes**. Cumbayá comes first, in the valley, and is often closer to 25–35 minutes. Do not add those together. You are going to one address. A city drop in the daytime is often quoted as 40–60 minutes because the same road clogs; 11:40pm is the lighter version, not a promise down to the minute. Wyndham Quito Airport is the Tababela hotel, typically 10–15 minutes, and it is the $50 product, not a stand-in for a hotel downtown.

If you are coming from sea-level Bogotá, Quito’s airport is already high and the city is higher. Headache and shortness of breath on the walk to the car are common on night one. Sit in the SUV. Do not add a fare argument to it.
