Few photos are as fun to send home as one foot in each hemisphere. Mitad del Mundo — "the Middle of the World" — is an easy, iconic half-day from Quito.

## What to see

The headline is the **Mitad del Mundo monument** in San Antonio de Pichincha, marking the equatorial line with a grand stone tower and a plaza of exhibits. Right nearby, the **Intiñan museum** is the local favorite: a hands-on, slightly cheeky tour with the famous water-drain and balancing-egg demonstrations on the "true" GPS equator.

With a little more time, pair it with the nearby **Pululahua crater** — a lush, inhabited volcanic caldera with a jaw-dropping viewpoint. (We have a [full Pululahua guide](/guide/pululahua-crater-guide).)

## Getting there from Quito airport (UIO)

Mitad del Mundo sits on the north side of the Quito area, about an hour's drive. From **UIO airport** in the eastern valley, that means a cross-region trip — straightforward with a private driver, more of a puzzle by public transport. ## Tips

- Go in the **morning** for clearer skies — and better Pululahua views before the clouds roll in.
- Bring **sun protection**: you're literally on the equator at altitude, and the UV is intense.
- It pairs well with a first or last day in Quito — close enough to do before an evening flight.
- Staying in the [Old Town](/guide/quito-old-town-guide) or [La Mariscal](/guide/la-mariscal-quito-guide)? It's an easy add-on either way.

Book ahead and the equator becomes a relaxed morning out, not a logistics exercise.
