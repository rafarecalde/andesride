The two most common ways into Quito from the airport are a regular taxi and a pre-booked private transfer. They look similar on the surface — a car and a driver — but the experience and the math are quite different.

## Price

- **Taxi:** Often cheaper on paper, but the fare to the city isn't fixed, meters aren't always used at the airport, and "airport surcharge" surprises are common. You usually don't know the final number until you arrive.
- **Private transfer:** A single **flat USD price, confirmed before you fly**. It's typically a premium over the cheapest taxi — deliberately so, because it pays for a properly licensed driver and removes the guesswork.

## Certainty and safety

- **Taxi:** You're choosing from the rank on arrival and trusting whatever turns up. Quality and legitimacy vary.
- **Private transfer:** A **vetted, airport-authorized** driver is booked in advance and waiting with your name on a sign. The price, the car, and the driver are all known quantities. ([Why that licensing matters.](/guide/why-licensed-airport-drivers-matter-quito))

## Hassle, especially after a flight

- **Taxi:** Negotiate a fare, possibly in Spanish, while tired and jet-lagged — and the line thins out late at night.
- **Private transfer:** Walk straight to your driver, who helps with bags and takes you door-to-door. Flight tracking means they're there whenever you actually land. ([How meet & greet works.](/guide/dont-arrive-to-an-empty-airport))

## Who each one suits

- **Choose a taxi** if you're on a tight budget, traveling light, arriving in daylight, and comfortable negotiating.
- **Choose a private transfer** if you're arriving at night, carrying luggage, traveling as a family, value a fixed price, or simply want the arrival handled. See the [prepaid rates](/#rates).

## The verdict

A taxi can save you a few dollars; a private transfer saves you the uncertainty — fixed price, vetted driver, no negotiation, no waiting.
