Quito's Old Town was one of the first UNESCO World Heritage Sites ever named — and walking it, you understand why. Cobbled streets, gilded churches, and grand plazas spread across the slopes beneath Pichincha volcano.

## What to see in Centro Histórico

- **La Compañía de Jesús** — a Baroque church dripping in gold leaf; genuinely breathtaking.
- **Plaza Grande (Plaza de la Independencia)** — the civic heart, flanked by the cathedral and presidential palace.
- **Basílica del Voto Nacional** — climb the towers for one of the best views in the city (not for the faint of heart).
- **El Panecillo** — the hilltop winged Virgin statue watching over the center.
- **La Ronda** — a restored lane of cafés, artisans, and live music in the evening.

Give it a full day, wear good shoes for the hills, and pace yourself — you're at roughly 2,850 m.

## Getting there from Quito airport (UIO)

The airport sits in Tababela, around 45 minutes to an hour from the historic center depending on traffic. From **UIO** a private transfer takes you door-to-door to your hotel — no transfers, no hauling bags through the bus system after a flight. With a **flat rate** you know the cost up front, and your driver tracks your flight and waits if you're delayed.

## Tips

- Base yourself in the Old Town for history and atmosphere, or nearby [La Mariscal](/guide/la-mariscal-quito-guide) for dining and nightlife.
- Take the **altitude** seriously on day one — those hills are steeper than they look.
- It's an easy launch pad for day trips to [Mitad del Mundo](/guide/visiting-mitad-del-mundo) and the [TelefériQo](/guide/teleferico-quito-pichincha).

Arrive with a driver already waiting and your first impression of Quito is the architecture — not the arrivals-hall taxi line.
