Money logistics are easy to overlook until you're standing at arrivals working out how to pay. Here's the quick version for Quito.

## Yes, it's US dollars

Ecuador's official currency is the **US dollar**, so prices — including airport transport — are in USD. There's no currency to exchange if you're coming from the States, which removes one common arrival headache. Bring **small bills**; breaking large notes can be hard, especially late at night.

## Cash vs. card

- **Taxis and many local services are cash-first.** Don't assume a curbside taxi takes cards, and airport ATMs can occasionally be out of cash on late arrivals.
- **Rideshare** charges your app's saved card (when pickup is allowed), but surge can make the final amount a surprise.
- **A pre-booked private transfer** is the cleanest: you **pay online by card up front**, in USD, and the price is fixed — so you arrive with nothing to settle at the curb. ([See the prepaid rates.](/#rates))

## Tipping

Tipping drivers isn't strictly required, but rounding up or a few dollars for good service — especially help with heavy bags — is appreciated. With a pre-paid transfer, any tip is entirely optional and separate from the fixed fare.

## The easiest setup for arrival

To avoid fumbling at arrivals after a long flight:

1. **Pre-book and pre-pay** your airport transfer so the ride is already settled.
2. Carry a little **USD cash in small bills** for tips and incidentals.
3. Make sure your **card and a backup** are accessible, and your data/eSIM works in case you need an app.
