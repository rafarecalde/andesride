If Quito's altitude has you a little breathless, Mindo is the perfect antidote: a lush, warm cloud forest northwest of the city where the air is thick with birdsong instead of thin with elevation.

## What to do in Mindo

Mindo sits at around 1,250 m, so it's noticeably greener and milder than Quito. It's one of the best **birdwatching** spots on the planet — hummingbirds by the dozen, and if you're lucky, the bright-orange Andean cock-of-the-rock. Beyond birds:

- Ride the **tarabita** (cable car) across the canyon to hike a trail of waterfalls.
- Tour a small-batch **chocolate** maker from cacao bean to bar.
- Go **ziplining**, **tubing** on the river, or visit a butterfly farm.

It works as a long day trip or, better, an overnight to catch the dawn chorus.

## Getting there from Quito airport (UIO)

Mindo is around two to two-and-a-half hours from Quito, descending the western slope of the Andes through changing cloud-forest scenery. From **UIO airport** the drive is a real journey — winding and beautiful — which is exactly why a **flat-rate private transfer** beats piecing together buses: one fixed price, a driver who handles the mountain road, and flight tracking if you're coming straight from arrivals.

## Practical tips

- Pack a **light rain layer** — it's cloud forest; brief showers are part of the charm.
- Bring **insect repellent** and quick-dry clothes for the waterfall trails.
- Mornings are best for birds; book an early start.
- Coming down from a high-altitude trip like [Papallacta](/guide/papallacta-hot-springs-guide) or [Cotopaxi](/guide/cotopaxi-national-park-day-trip)? Mindo's lower elevation is a gentle, welcome contrast.

A pre-booked ride means you spend the day chasing hummingbirds, not bus schedules.
