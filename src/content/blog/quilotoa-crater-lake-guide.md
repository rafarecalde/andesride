Round a final bend in the highlands and the ground simply falls away into a vast caldera filled with impossibly turquoise water. Quilotoa is the kind of view that stops conversations.

## What to expect

Quilotoa is a water-filled volcanic crater high in the Andes southwest of Quito, sitting at around 3,900 m on the rim. The classic visit:

- Take in the **rim viewpoint** — the payoff is immediate and spectacular.
- **Hike down** to the water's edge (around 30–45 minutes); the climb back up is the hard part at this altitude — mules are available.
- Serious walkers use it as the finale of the multi-day **Quilotoa Loop** through Andean villages.

The light and color shift through the day, and the air is thin and cold, so it's a place to take your time.

## Getting there from Quito

Quilotoa is a few hours southwest of Quito through farmland and high páramo. ## Tips

- The rim is at high altitude — **don't rush the hike**, and acclimatize in Quito or the valleys first.
- Bring **warm, windproof layers**; it's cold and exposed on the rim even when sunny.
- Go **early** for the best light and to leave time for the climb back up.
- Pair it with other highlands stops on the way — just ask when you book.

With the driving handled by a pre-booked driver, you arrive fresh and spend your energy on the view, not the logistics.
