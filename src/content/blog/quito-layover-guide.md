A long layover in Quito doesn't have to mean staring at a departures board. The airport is far from the city, but with a pre-booked driver you can turn dead time into a real glimpse of Ecuador — and still make your onward flight.

## First, the one thing that makes it work

UIO sits in Tababela, 45–60 minutes from central Quito, so the math only works if your ground transport is sorted. A **private flat-rate transfer** with **flight tracking** is what makes a layover trip safe: your driver knows your schedule, watches your inbound flight, and gets you back to the terminal with time to spare. Trying to do this with curbside taxis is how people miss connections.

## ~6 hours: stay close

With a short window, don't gamble on city traffic. Stay in the **valley near the airport**:

- A relaxed meal and a stroll in **[Cumbayá](/guide/cumbaya-tumbaco-guide)**, just 25–35 minutes away.
- Or simply a comfortable [airport-area hotel](/guide/where-to-stay-near-quito-airport) day room to reset between flights.

## ~12 hours: see the highlights

Enough time for the city or one close sight:

- **[Quito's Old Town](/guide/quito-old-town-guide)** — gilded churches, grand plazas, and the Panecillo viewpoint.
- **[Mitad del Mundo](/guide/visiting-mitad-del-mundo)** — stand on the equator and be back in a few hours.

## ~24 hours: a proper day out

Now a real day trip is on the table:

- **[Otavalo's market](/guide/things-to-do-in-otavalo)** (especially on a Saturday).
- **[Papallacta's hot springs](/guide/papallacta-hot-springs-guide)** for a restorative soak.
- **[Mindo](/guide/mindo-cloud-forest-guide)** if you want to trade altitude for cloud forest.

## Layover tips

- Always **build in a buffer** — leave the city well before you think you need to.
- Keep your **passport and onward boarding pass** on you.
- Remember the **altitude** (2,850 m in the city) — go easy if you've been flying for hours.
- Book the transfer **round trip** so the same driver returns you to the terminal on schedule.
