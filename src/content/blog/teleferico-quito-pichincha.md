For the best panorama of Quito — the city stretching for miles along its high valley — head up the **TelefériQo**, one of the highest aerial cable cars in the world.

## What to expect

The gondola climbs the flank of Pichincha volcano to **Cruz Loma**, at around 4,050 m. From the top you get:

- A staggering **viewpoint** over Quito and, on clear days, a ring of distant volcanoes including Cotopaxi.
- Short walking paths, swings, and photo spots along the ridge.
- The trailhead for the hike up **Rucu Pichincha** (around 4,700 m) — a demanding half-day for acclimatized, well-prepared walkers only.

Even if you just ride up and back, the altitude jump is dramatic — go slowly and savor it.

## Getting there from Quito airport (UIO)

The cable-car base is on the **west side of Quito**, so from **UIO** in the eastern valley it's a drive across the city. A private transfer takes you straight there or to your hotel first; a **flat rate** means no meter through Quito traffic, and your driver can wait or collect you afterward.

It falls within the prepaid $100 [anywhere in Quito](/quito-airport-to-quito) rate — see that page, or the [rates](/#rates).

## Tips

- Save it for **after you've acclimatized** — 4,000 m hits hard if you just landed.
- Go on a **clear morning**; afternoons cloud over fast.
- Bring a **warm layer** — it's much colder at the top than in the city.
- Don't attempt Rucu Pichincha alone or unacclimatized; it's a serious mountain.

A pre-booked ride means you spend your energy on the view from the top, not on crossing the city to reach it.
