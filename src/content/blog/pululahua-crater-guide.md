Most volcanic craters are barren. Pululahua is the opposite — a vast green caldera with farms, forest, and a microclimate of its own, all just north of Quito and a short hop from the equator.

## What to see

Pululahua is a geobotanical reserve set inside a collapsed volcano. The easy win is the **Ventanillas viewpoint** on the rim, where the whole patchwork crater floor opens up below you. With more time and energy you can:

- **Hike down** into the crater and back (a real workout on the return).
- Visit the small farming community that actually lives inside the caldera.
- Combine it with nearby thermal pools and the equator sights.

Because it's so close to **Mitad del Mundo**, the two pair perfectly into one half- or full-day outing.

## Getting there from Quito airport (UIO)

Pululahua sits on the north side of the Quito area, near San Antonio de Pichincha. From **UIO** in the eastern valley it's a cross-region drive — easy with a private driver who can fold in Mitad del Mundo as an **extra stop**. A **flat rate** keeps the combined trip simple and priced up front.

Pululahua sits with Mitad del Mundo, outside the three prepaid products, so [ask for a quote](/#book). Published rates ($50 airport hotel / $75 Cumbayá / $100 Quito) are on the [rates page](/#rates).

## Tips

- Go in the **morning** — clouds often fill the crater by afternoon and hide the view.
- The rim is at altitude and the hike down is deceptively tough; **pace yourself**.
- Bundle it with [Mitad del Mundo](/guide/visiting-mitad-del-mundo) for a satisfying north-of-Quito day.

Book one driver for both stops and you get the equator and a living volcano in a single, easy outing.
