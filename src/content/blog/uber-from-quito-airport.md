"Can I just take an Uber from Quito airport?" is one of the most-searched travel questions about UIO — and the answer is a frustrating *it depends*. Here's the real picture.

## How rideshare works at UIO

Rideshare apps (Uber, inDrive, and others) operate in Quito, but the airport is a special case:

- **Airport pickup is restricted.** Designated transport has priority at arrivals, and rideshare pickup rules at UIO have changed over time and can be enforced unevenly. You may be asked to meet your driver away from the main arrivals area.
- **Surge pricing** spikes fares at busy arrival banks and late at night — exactly when most international flights land.
- **No one is waiting.** You request on arrival, then wait, watch the app, and hope the driver finds you — with your luggage, possibly after midnight, in an unfamiliar airport.
- **Connectivity matters.** If your eSIM or data isn't working the moment you land, you can't even book.

## When rideshare makes sense — and when it doesn't

Off-peak, in daylight, with working data and light luggage, a rideshare into the city can be cheaper. But the savings shrink with surge pricing, and the **uncertainty is the real cost**: this is the moment travelers most often overpay, get into the wrong car, or stand around stranded.

## The alternative: a flat rate that's set before you land

A private transfer removes every one of those variables. A vetted, **airport-authorized** driver is already waiting at arrivals with your name, the **price is fixed in USD before you fly**, and your flight is tracked so a delay doesn't matter. ## Bottom line

Rideshare *can* work at UIO on a good day — but it's the option with the most asterisks. For a night landing, a family, or anyone who values certainty over saving a few dollars, a pre-booked flat-rate transfer is the calmer choice.
